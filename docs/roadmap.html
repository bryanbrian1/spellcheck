This page is a snapshot; the other two are the authority

CLAUDE.md holds the constraints and outranks everything. The blueprint describes the architecture. This page describes only the remaining work, measured on 7 September 2026, and it goes stale the moment any of it is done — so treat a claim here as a lead to verify, not a fact to build on.

The unknown that dominated this project is gone

Until now every layer that touches a running game — the LCU client, the champ select socket, the live data poll, the suggestions, the matchup path — was unit-tested and had never met League. That was the single largest source of risk, and it has been discharged by playing a game. What replaced it is five ordinary bugs, which is a far better position. A sixth has since come out of fixing one of them.

01Six defects, five from the first real game

The first five came out of one session against the real client. Four are traced to a specific line; the gold one was diagnosed from a captured game. They are listed hardest-to-diagnose first, not most-severe first. The sixth arrived differently — it was found on 7 September while auditing an abandoned branch, and the fix to defect 5 is what made it visible.

Defect 1

The gold number is wrong — fixed

Diagnosed from a captured game, 6 September. 81 samples across one 40-minute match, taken on the app’s own 30-second cadence. The earlier guess on this page — that consumables and trinkets were inflating the total — was wrong, and is recorded here rather than deleted because the way it was wrong is instructive. The captured game ended with no consumables in either inventory at all, so that theory could not have explained anything.

The live API’s price is the combine cost, not the item’s cost

It is exactly Data Dragon’s gold.base, never gold.total. Summing it counts only the last purchase in each item’s build path and silently discards every component already consumed into it.

Three readings from the capture settle it beyond argument, checked against Data Dragon 16.17.1:

ItemAPI pricegold.basegold.total
B. F. Sword — a base component130013001300
Blackfire Torch7007002800
Void Staff105010503000
Rabadon’s Deathcap110011003500
Chainlaced Crushers — finished boots001100

A base component reports its true cost, because for a base item the combine cost is the total — which is precisely why this survived review. Every completed item reports only its final step, and a finished pair of boots reports zero. A full six-item Viktor build summed to 3,950 gold.

What it did to the feature

46%Your spend was counted at 46% of its real value, averaged across the game.
37%The standing disagreed with the truth in 30 of 81 samples — behind, even or ahead was simply the wrong word.
18%You were genuinely behind and the app said nothing for 15 samples. Behind is the only footing that produces advice, so the check was silent in exactly the window it exists to serve.
16×It reported ahead when you were not in 16 samples — the reading that tells a player to keep doing what they are doing.

The error is biased, not noisy

It does not average out. A player holding raw components is counted near-correctly; a player who has finished their items is counted at a quarter of the truth. So the comparison systematically favours whoever has completed fewer items — which is usually the player who is behind. The check was not merely inaccurate, it was leaning the wrong way, and worst exactly when the gap was widest: 2,950 gold of error at 31 minutes.

The fix

The app needs gold.total per item, which the live payload never carries. data/meta/items.json already holds a committed cost field derived from Data Dragon — exactly the right shape and already compiled in with include_str! — but it covers only the 75 items in the defensive vocabulary, and none of the five above are among them. Widening that table to all 868 items is the whole fix, and it needs no new network call, no new dependency, and no change to how the tags already ship.

Done — and it uncovered a second bug on the way

The table now carries all 868 items at gold.total, regenerated by npm run meta:items. Pricing moved into recommend/state.rs, which is the only layer allowed to know what an item costs, and price is no longer read at all.

Wiring the table up immediately failed every lookup, which exposed something that had been latent for months: the payload field is itemID with both letters capitalised, and rename_all = "camelCase" renders it itemId, so item_id had been silently deserialising as zero the entire time. Nothing had ever read it, so nothing had ever noticed. It needed an explicit rename.

The scrubbed capture is committed at src-tauri/src/live/testdata/allgamedata.json and drives a regression test that asserts both the corrected verdict and the old one: the same payload reads ahead by 1,800 from the table and even by 800 from price. No hand-written fixture could have caught this, because the bug was in what a field means.

Two things still to settle. Sold and consumed purchases are still missing from any inventory sum, so “spent gold” remains an approximation even once the arithmetic is right — worth deciding whether the label should say so. And an item absent from the table must count as unknown rather than zero, or a future item will reintroduce this bug in miniature and just as quietly.

Why 219 passing tests never saw it

The stand-in client mints items as { itemID, price, count } with a price the test itself chooses, so every assertion agreed with an arithmetic that was never in question. The real payload carries nine fields per item — canUse, consumable, count, displayName, itemID, price, rawDescription, rawDisplayName, slot — and the bug lives entirely in what price means, which no fixture invented by the same person who wrote the parser could ever contradict. A captured payload is the only thing that could have caught this, and now there is one.

Defect 2

Opening the app mid-game — not reproduced, one real fault fixed

The path exists and is deliberate. This is not a missing feature — it was built on purpose, with a comment saying so: opening the app after champ select is over is an ordinary way to use it, and the game names the same two things champ select does.

src-tauri/src/lib.rs:612 — “Opening the app after champ select is over is an ordinary way to use it, and until now it meant playing the whole game with no build on screen.”

The gate is probably not the problem. The live watcher sleeps on a watch channel that opens on any champ-select event other than ClientOffline, and ClientConnected is sent as soon as the socket connects — which happens mid-game, because the launcher stays running behind the game. That sequence looks correct on inspection.

src-tauri/src/lib.rs:489 — launcher_running.send(!matches!(&event, ClientOffline))
src-tauri/src/lcu/watcher.rs:156 — events.send(ChampSelectEvent::ClientConnected)

Four hypotheses, all disproven by evidence. This entry previously blamed champion-key resolution through the tag file. That was wrong, and so was everything else tried:

WorksChampion resolution. The captured payload runs through in_game_state and yields champion Viktor, key Viktor, role middle, opponent Velkoz. Pinned by a test.
WorksThe gate. ClientConnected fires on connect and opens it, and a watch channel delivers a change regardless of which task races first.
WorksThe champ-select read. A live client outside champ select answers 404, which the client already reads as Ok(None) rather than an error.
WorksThe matchup vocabulary. Checked live against the endpoint: Viktor–Vel’Koz, plus Wukong, Bel’Veth, Cho’Gath and Nunu. All five return matchup builds.
WorksThe whole route, end to end. Driven against a stand-in serving a game with no champ select before it: the app connected, opened the gate, polled the live API and fetched a build from OP.GG unprompted.

One real fault, and it is unique to this route

Naming an opponent used to be a single point of failure. fetch_build called the matchup tool with ? and had no fallback, so a matchup that errored — or simply had too few games for an uncommon pairing and returned NoData — took the whole build down with it.

Champ select never hit this, because the client blanks enemy positions and it cannot name an opponent. The in-game route always can, so it always asked. That is exactly one route showing nothing useful while the other works, which is the reported symptom — but it is not confirmed as what was seen, because it depends on the pairing and the pairing is unknown.

Fixed: a failed or empty matchup now falls back to the general build, which is what commands.rs already documented (“naming one is only a request”) and what the screen was already built to render.

The route had never been exercised by anything

Not a test, not the stand-in client, which always opens with champ select. A documented, supported way of using the app had never once been driven. node scripts/fake-lcu.mjs --mid-game now starts a match with no champ select before it, which is how everything above was ruled out — and is what anyone should reach for the next time this is reported.

Still open. If it recurs, the thing to capture is the champion and the lane opponent, because the fallback above is the only failure mode that fits and it is pairing-dependent.

Defect 3

The build never goes away after the match — fixed

This one is working as designed, and the design is wrong. The noGame branch deliberately leaves the build on screen, with a stated rationale.

src/main.ts:1272 — “The build in particular stays: the minutes after a game are exactly when someone reads what they should have built.”

The reasoning is sound and the result is still wrong, because the screen makes no distinction between a build for the game you are playing and a build for the game you just played. Those are the same pixels making two different claims, and the second one silently becomes a lie the moment the next champ select starts.

So the fix is not to clear it. Deleting the build would discard the exact case the comment is protecting. What is missing is a signal that the thing on screen is finished — a state on the header, the live pill reading something other than a live state, the standing banner retiring since it describes a game that has ended. Keep the build, mark it over.

Done — the build stays, the impression of a live game does not

A finished reading is held in lastGame, so the header can say which game it is describing: the pill reads Game over and the subtitle “Mid · game ended”, following the shape champ select ending already uses. clearLive drops it, so a fresh champ select still opens clean.

The blocks split on tense rather than all persisting or all vanishing. The standing and the advice beside it describe a game that is running — “Vel’Koz is 2,400 gold up on you” is a fact with a tense — so they drop at the whistle. Check one describes the enemy composition, which the final whistle does not change, so it stays. It now stays as the game’s reading too: reverting to champ select’s partial view at the end would have quietly swapped a complete answer for a worse one.

Verified by driving the built main.js through lock → build → playing → ended → next champ select against a stubbed bridge, rather than by reading the types.

The BuildClaim concern raised here earlier was unfounded: champ select takes the claim unconditionally on every lock, so the next game re-fetches even on the same champion. Nothing to release.

Defect 4

Situational shows several items and explains none of them — fixed

What is rendered. “Situational” arrives as several groups of one item each — a menu of alternatives — and is drawn as a row of tiles under a header that counts them. The header text is literally the count.

src/main.ts:547 — itemRowBlock(…)
src/main.ts:563 — const meta = single ? statLine(first.stats) : `${shown.length} options`

Each tile carries its own win rate in a tooltip, so the numbers are honest and per-item. What no tile carries is the only thing a player actually needs: when to buy that one rather than the one beside it. Six options with no discriminator is not six times as useful as one recommendation; it is roughly as useful as none.

The app already owns the missing sentences — a suggestion is { itemId, priority, reason, source }, and the recommendation engine produces exactly the reasoning that would disambiguate this row. It is rendered in separate blocks (“Against this team”, “For your team”) rather than against the items it is talking about.

The colour law makes this a design problem, not a text problem

The situational row is statistical — teal, drawn from the provider’s game counts. A reason is a rule — amber, and it argues in words. Dropping engine reasons into a teal block would let reasoning borrow a statistic’s authority, which is the precise failure the colour law exists to prevent, and painting the whole row amber would strip real win rates of theirs. Joining the two needs a rendering that keeps the sources visibly distinct, not a caption.

The cheaper half is available immediately and breaks no rule: the provider’s own numbers can order the row, and a group played in 3 games out of 98 probably should not sit beside one played in 40 as though the app were neutral between them. Saturation already encodes confidence elsewhere via railFor(); the same idea applies per tile.

Defect 5

Three empty slots on the rune page — fixed

They are not extra slots. They are the stat shards, and they are the best-understood defect on this page — two lines explain the whole thing.

No name. runeBlock passes undefined where every other tile passes a name, so tileLabel falls through to its last resort and prints the raw id.

src/main.ts:491 — tile(id, undefined, "tile sm", "perk")
src/main.ts:367 — name?.trim() ? … : `#${id}`

No art either. Icon art for a rune comes from icons.perks[id], which is built from Data Dragon’s runesReforged.json — and that file does not contain stat shards. The lookup misses, iconUrl returns null, and paintIcons returns early without ever inserting an image.

src/main.ts:311 — const path = icons.perks[key]; return path ? … : null

So each shard draws as a 24px box containing the literal text #5005 at 8px. Three of them, in a row, after the secondary runes — which reads exactly like three empty slots that should not be there.

The comment describes behaviour the code does not have

paintIcons is documented as leaving shards their text label permanently, and that is the right design — a tile with no art should fall back to words. But runeBlock never supplies the words, so the fallback lands one step further down on #<id>. The design was sound and the wiring was never finished.

Done — and they get real art, not just a label

The premise that shards have no art was wrong. Data Dragon does serve it, at perk-images/StatMods/… on the same unversioned path as every other rune image and from a host the window’s policy already allows — it just omits the shards from runesReforged.json, which lists only styles and the runes inside their slots. So the catalogue that file produces is incomplete by construction, and STAT_SHARDS in ddragon.rs completes it. All nine URLs were checked live.

The catalogue now also carries a name for every rune, style and shard, which is what a tile shows when art is slow, blocked or absent. paintIcons fills those in when the catalogue lands, so a page rendered before it arrives corrects itself rather than keeping an id on screen.

The sizing question resolved itself honestly: a 24px tile cannot render “Adaptive Force” at any size worth having, and shrinking the type to make it fit is what produced the 9px captions the redesign removed. The label stays a fallback, the full name lives in the tile’s title, and art carries the meaning.

Defect 6

The rune labels print through the art — fixed

A tile paints its image over its label so that art which never arrives leaves the words it stood in for. That is deliberate, and it is stated in the stylesheet. It is also only true of items, whose art is an opaque square that covers the words underneath.

src/styles.css:147 — .tile img{position:absolute;inset:0;…object-fit:cover}
src/styles.css:145 — /* Art sits over the label, so a tile whose image never arrives still reads as the words it always did. */

Rune art is a transparent symbol, not an opaque square. So the label never stopped showing — it printed through the gaps in the glyph, and the tile read as a rendering fault rather than as a rune. paintIcons carries an error handler that removes a broken image, but nothing that retires the label when one succeeds, so the words stay behind the art forever.

src/main.ts:357 — img.addEventListener("error", () => img.remove()) — and no "load" beside it

Defect 5 did not cause this. It made it impossible to miss. Giving the stat shards real art put nine transparent PNGs onto 24px tiles whose labels are still underneath, and a shard glyph leaves most of its tile empty. Measured on the asset Data Dragon actually serves:

StatModsAdaptiveForceIcon.pngValue
Fully transparent pixels83% of 32×32
Fully transparent across the band where a centred label sits57%

So the three shards read as tiles with garbage behind them — Adapti, Attack Sp, Armor. Every rune tile has this, the keystone included; the shards are only where it stopped being deniable. Confirmed in champ select against a running client, not just in a harness.

An abandoned branch had already diagnosed it, on 5 September

rune-page-shape names the cause exactly — “item art is an opaque square and hides the label; rune art is a transparent symbol, so the words behind it came through the gaps” — and carries the fix. It was never merged because its other half was wrong: it claimed the shards have no art anywhere, which defect 5 later disproved. The correct diagnosis went down with the incorrect one, and the bug it described was then widened by the commit that superseded it.

That is the cost of judging a branch by its weakest claim. Nothing was re-derived here that had not already been written down two days earlier.

Both halves worth keeping have since been recovered. The label fix landed with this defect, and the branch’s other sound observation — that a rune page is three things drawn as two, with the secondary tree named nowhere — is a separate change. Only the claim about shard art was left behind, which is the one thing about that branch that was actually wrong.

Done — the label stands down once art has actually arrived

The label stands down once art has actually loaded, on load rather than eagerly, so art that fails or never comes still leaves the words exactly where they were. That path was checked by pointing a tile at a 404: the image removes itself, the flag is never set, the label keeps its full height. The full name is on the tile’s title either way, which is what defect 5’s sizing decision already assumed.

It needed the label to be an element rather than a bare text node, and wrapping it closed a latent bug on the way. paintIcons corrects a tile still showing #8112 by assigning to holder.textContent, which deletes every child — including an <img> prepended by an earlier pass. Any second pass over a tile whose id had not resolved silently threw its art away.

src/main.ts:343 — holder.textContent = tileLabelText(name) — wipes a prepended image

No test covers it. The repo has no frontend harness — the ingest tests are Node, everything else is Rust — and building one means a DOM dependency that CLAUDE.md asks be justified before adding. It was verified by hand against the real client and against the compiled dist/styles.css, and that limitation is recorded rather than left implied.

02The crawled data is not a dataset yet

The crawl works, has run against the live API, and has committed real output — the blueprint’s claim that this is untested is stale. What it has produced is not yet enough to stand behind. Measured directly over data/builds/:

A median of ten games behind the headline recommendation is still far below the threshold this project already set for itself — the crawler’s own notes call a few dozen games per pair “below the threshold at which this app is willing to call a number a statistic”. Fewer than a third of pairs clear both bars. RiotProvider can answer; it should not yet be the one answering.

The direction, though, is right. One overnight crawl moved the median pair from 44 games to 63.5 and the top core group from 8 to 10, and cut the pairs under 30 games from 109 to 95. Accumulation works and is running unattended; the only question left is arithmetic, not plumbing.

The key does not expire daily — and never did

This page previously called a daily-expiring development key the single highest-leverage item on it, and said accumulation could not happen unless somebody rotated a key each morning. That was wrong, and it is corrected here rather than deleted because the shape of the error matters: it was inferred from how the crawler was built, never measured against the running system.

The secret was set once, on 4 September 23:31 UTC, and its updated_at did not move again for three days. Scheduled crawls succeeded on 5, 6 and 7 September regardless. A 24-hour development key cannot do that, and nobody was rotating one by hand. A non-expiring key was already in place.

The key was rotated anyway on 7 September as hygiene, and verified live: check-key.mjs passed against the new secret on a dispatched run before the crawl was cancelled. Nothing downstream was ever waiting on any of this.

What the key never fixed is the arithmetic. The rate limit is what shapes the whole crawler — the 45-minute clock, the decision to buy a timeline per match, the accumulate-don’t-replace design. With a patch cycle resetting the sample roughly every two weeks by design, the open question is whether the popular pairs reach significance before the reset and the rare ones ever do. Three honest answers exist: a narrower crawl that goes deep on fewer pairs, accepting that OP.GG stays the live provider indefinitely and the crawl is a hedge rather than a migration, or letting one full patch cycle accumulate and deciding on the evidence. The third is now the cheap one — the crawl runs unattended, so the cost of waiting is nothing but time, and it is the only option that answers the question rather than guessing at it. That decision has not been made and is worth making explicitly.

03Between a beta and something you hand a stranger

MissingItem set and rune page imports. Four buttons are permanently disabled, and the LCU client deliberately contains no code that could write. Needs the summoner id, a POST to /lol-item-sets/… and /lol-perks/v1/pages, and the button wiring. This is the only item on the page a user would call a missing feature.
MissingSelf-update. An installed copy cannot fetch a new version; every release is a manual download. Needs tauri-plugin-updater, a signing keypair and a manifest endpoint.
Half doneCode signing. macOS is already wired — adding the six APPLE_* secrets turns it on with no workflow edit, for $99/yr. Windows needs a certificate and has no equivalent shortcut. Until then both systems warn on first open.
MissingA settings UI. CLAUDE.md calls SPELLCHECK_LOCKFILE a user-facing setting on Windows, but the only ways to set it are an environment variable or a hand-edited providers.json in an OS config directory — which is exactly the population least equipped to do either.
MissingProvider fallback. If OP.GG is down or changes format the app has no answer, even though data/builds ships inside the bundle as a resource. The trait already makes this a small change.
MissingA version-agreement check. The tag must match three files and nothing verifies it; the README says so in prose. Ten lines of CI.
UnmeasuredMemory on Windows. The under-120 MB budget was set against WKWebView; Windows renders in WebView2, which runs more processes. CLAUDE.md forbids repeating the macOS number for Windows until somebody measures it, and nobody has.
UnprovenWindows on real hardware. It compiles in CI and produces an NSIS installer. No human has run it. The Mac cannot tell you otherwise — ring builds C, so there is no cross-compile.

04Named holes in the engine

Each of these is small, well understood, and already documented where it lives. None is urgent; all are cheap.

StaleThree untagged champions — Locke, Yunara, Zaahen. The tag file is pinned at patch 16.17.1 and every new release joins this list. See defect 2 for why this currently costs more than it should.
MissingTwo answers nothing can ask for. Shield reduction and crit reduction have items behind them but no champion tag that would trigger them, so Serpent’s Fang and Randuin’s Omen are never suggested. Closing this means adding a champion tag, not an item.
ThinCheck two emits one rule. Widening it requires adding an answers entry to data/meta/items.json first and a rule reaching for it second. The blueprint is emphatic that inventing advice to fill the space is the failure mode here.
FragileThe OP.GG vocabulary table. The two OP.GG tools disagree about champion names, and every new two-word-name champion needs a manual entry. The symptom is one champion failing while every other works — which is the hardest possible symptom to notice.

06What to do first

Ordered by what each step unblocks rather than by size.

  1. Rotate to a non-expiring Riot key. Was never blocking. The key had not been expiring daily since at least 4 September and three unattended crawls prove it; it was rotated on 7 September as hygiene and verified live through the check-key gate. Nothing in section 02 was waiting behind it, which makes the open question there a decision to take, not a task to schedule.
  2. Widen data/meta/items.json to carry gold.total for every item. Done. All 868 items, pricing moved to the tag layer, a latent itemID deserialisation bug fixed on the way, and a real captured payload committed as the regression test.
  3. Re-point the stand-in client at the real payload shape. Done. scripts/fake-lcu.mjs now mints all nine fields with real ids and real combine costs, so a scenario can no longer invent a price the parser agrees with.
  4. Investigate mid-game detection. Not reproduced. Four hypotheses disproven and the route driven end to end against a new --mid-game stand-in. One real fault fixed on the way: a failed matchup no longer takes the whole build with it.
  5. Mark a finished game as finished. Done. The build survives the whistle, but the header now says the game is over and the live-only blocks retire.
  6. Name the stat shards. Done. Nine rows in ddragon.rs giving them Riot’s own art and a name, plus names for every other rune so a tile without art still says what it is.
  7. Sort the situational row by sample, then design the reason rendering. Done. Named columns with their own numbers, ordered by sample, and the engine’s reasoning joined to them in amber without either claim borrowing the other’s authority.
  8. Stop the rune labels printing through the art. Done. Found on 7 September, not in the first game — defect 5’s art made it visible, and an abandoned branch had diagnosed it two days earlier. Hand-verified against the live client; no test, because there is no frontend harness to put one in.
  9. Draw a rune page as three rows, and name the secondary tree. Open. The other sound half of that abandoned branch: the secondary runes and the stat shards shared a row, and which tree to open for the second pair was stated nowhere on screen. The ids were already in the payload and already in the type; nothing read them.
  10. Get the Windows build onto real hardware and measure its memory. Two unknowns, one tester, one afternoon. With the defect list closed this is now the top item, and it is the only thing standing between the current state and an honest beta claim.
  11. Clear the cheap backlog in section 04. Under an hour together: the version-agreement CI check (all three files currently agree at 0.1.1 and match tag v0.1.1, so this is preventive rather than a live break) and the three untagged champions, still Locke, Yunara and Zaahen, re-verified against the crawl on 7 September.
  12. Then imports. The largest genuinely missing feature, and the first thing on this list that is a project rather than a fix.

Imports stay user-initiated, however convenient automatic would be

When step 8 arrives: writing an item set or a rune page into the client is always a button press. Never automatic, never on a timer, never as a side effect of a lookup. The read-only LCU client is not an accident of scheduling — it is the rule expressed as an absence of code, and the first write path is where that rule stops enforcing itself.
